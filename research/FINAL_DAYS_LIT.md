# Final-Days Mispricing & Liquidity Literature Review

**Question:** In the final 3-9 days before the SIG Nov 4 venue lock, should the settlement-book short-the-tails strategy become MORE aggressive (size/coverage/capital) or MORE conservative, based on real evidence (not speculation) about how mispricing, liquidity, and panic-selling behave near binary-event resolution?

**Bottom line:** The strongest, most structurally analogous evidence (Rothschild & Pennock 2014; Zitzewitz 2026 NBER; Clinton & Huang 2025) says cross-venue mispricing of exactly our kind does **not** reliably shrink near resolution and can **grow** right up to the event — but only in **capital/position-constrained** venues, which SIG structurally resembles far more than it resembles Polymarket/Betfair. Liquidity-degradation evidence is real but concentrated in **acute final hours** (election night chaos), not a multi-day decay — and since we hold to true resolution rather than being forced to exit at the venue lock, that risk hits *entry fill quality*, not forced liquidation. Net: lean more aggressive, front-loaded into the earlier part of the remaining window, expressed mainly as **wider coverage** (more illiquid tail markets) rather than concentrating further into already-held names.

---

## Q1. Does the favorite-longshot bias (FLB) widen, narrow, or stay flat as resolution approaches?

**The literature's single unifying rule:** mispricing shrinks into resolution when arbitrage capital is **unconstrained**; it persists or grows when arbitrage is **capital- or position-constrained**. This conditioning variable, not a generic "markets are/aren't efficient" prior, decides the answer.

**Unconstrained-market evidence (bias shrinks):**
- **Page & Clemen (2013)**, "Do Prediction Markets Produce Well-Calibrated Probability Forecasts?" *Economic Journal* 123(568):491-513. Intrade data, no position caps. Abstract: "time until expiration... should negatively affect the accuracy of prices in the direction of a favourite/longshot bias... markets are reasonably well calibrated when time to expiration is relatively short, but prices are significantly biased for events farther in the future." Political markets specifically: price 0.20 → actual frequency **10.9%**; price 0.80 → actual **92.8%** (far-dated). Split at 100 days to expiration. **Critical caveat:** mechanism is time-discounting/asymmetric trader withdrawal, explicitly **not** information arrival — "the improvement in calibration over time can be explained without appealing to information effects" — and the model does not claim to explain behavior in the final days or near the 0/1 extremes.
- **Erikson & Wlezien (2008)**, "Are Political Markets Really Superior to Polls?" *POQ* 72(2):190-215. IEM vote-share market vs. *properly debiased* polls: "only toward the end of the campaign does the market catch up to the polls... as Election Day approaches there is little new for the market to anticipate." Within final 30 days, prices fall in line with vote projections. (Different comparison object — convergence to true probability, not cross-exchange arbitrage.)
- **Berg, Nelson & Rietz**, "Prediction Market Accuracy in the Long Run," *Int'l J. of Forecasting* 24(2):285-300: >100 days out, poll error 4.49pp vs. market error 2.65pp; within 5 days of the election, poll error 1.62pp vs. market error 1.11pp — the market's *relative* edge over polls is proportionally largest far out, smaller (not absent) very close in.

**Constrained-market evidence (bias does NOT shrink, sometimes grows) — more structurally relevant to SIG:**
- **Zitzewitz (2026)**, "Limits to Arbitrage and Prediction Market Efficiency: the Case of PredictIt," NBER WP 35845. PredictIt enforced **$850 position caps**. Table 8 directly tests bias at 1-day/1-week/4-week/8-week/16-week snapshots before the election: **"the longshot bias does not decline significantly as the election approaches"** — explicitly contrasted with Page & Clemen's Intrade result. Mechanism: caps bind *more* over time as arbitrageurs fill up ("arbitrageurs... face limits on downside risk that might become more binding over time"). Betfair (no caps, in the same paper) shows "essentially no overpricing." When PredictIt's cap was quadrupled in 2025 and volume fell 10-15x, "the longshot bias has disappeared" — direct causal confirmation that cap-to-volume ratio, not time per se, determines correction. Also: Trump-reelection-pays-$1 contracts traded **9-16 cents for weeks** after Nov 3, 2020 during contested counting.
- **Clinton & Huang (2025)**, "Prediction Markets? The Accuracy and Efficiency of $2.4 Billion in the 2024 Presidential Election," SocArXiv. 2,500+ markets, IEM/Kalshi/PredictIt/Polymarket, final 5 weeks of 2024: **"the opportunity for arbitrage actually grows in the last two weeks before converging to zero on Election Day once the actual vote begins to be reported. The fact that the opportunity for arbitrage increases rather than decreases in the end is contrary to what should be observed by traders seeking to maximize their profit."** Arbitrage present 62 of 65 days. Separately: presidential race-outcome accuracy is **highest more than a week out and declines approaching Election Day** (core race only; pooled accuracy across all contract types does rise near Nov).
- **Cardozo & Rivero-Wildemauwe (2026)**, arXiv:2609.12878, Polymarket, 588M trades/2.48M accounts. Table 14, returns by time-to-close: pooled longshot return is **−10.86%** for purchases ≤1 day before close vs. **−19.35%** full-sample — i.e., messy/non-monotonic and aggregation-method-dependent, not a clean "worse at the very end" story either way.

**Historical/mechanistic context:**
- **Rhode & Strumpf (2004)**, "Historical Presidential Betting Markets," *JEP* 18(2):127-141. 1912: Wilson/Roosevelt/Taft prices **summed to more than $1 for the final 10 days** — a documented arbitrage-violation window in the final stretch, pre-1940s. 1916: a single $60,000 Ohio bet moved odds **~10 percentage points** in the final weeks (correctly, on Wilson). The 1916 market **did not settle until November 23** — two-plus weeks after voting — because results were unclear; directly analogous to a "deadline before true resolution" structure.
- **Snowberg & Wolfers (2010)**, *JPE* 118(4):723-746. Explicitly **overturns** the older claim (Asch, Malkiel & Quandt 1982) that bias worsens at an intra-day deadline analog ("last race of the day"): "If there was evidence of loss aversion in earlier data, it is no longer evident in recent data, even as the favorite-longshot bias has persisted." Frames FLB as a stable structural feature.
- **Shin (1991/1992/1993)** insider-trading/bookmaker-spread models (Economic Journal): establish that bias magnitude increases in *z*, the proportion of insider bettors. **Flag:** the specific claim that this implies bias mechanically widens near post-time could not be verified against Shin's primary text (paywalled) — treat as plausible inference, not a confirmed finding.
- **Asch, Malkiel & Quandt (1982)** / **Crafts (1985)**: horse-racing "late/smart money" — starting price (post-time) is more accurate than the morning line, because informed bettors enter late. Suggests narrowing *between early quotes and the literal close* specifically on the contracts informed money targets.
- **Ottaviani & Sørensen (2010)**, *AEJ: Micro* 2(1):58-85: formalizes why — expected return on an outcome rises with the fraction of money on it; bias magnitude is an endogenous function of the private-info/noise ratio. Late informed money sharpens pricing on the specific contracts insiders trade, while simultaneously raising adverse-selection risk for anyone market-making broadly.

**Read for SIG:** a fixed-pool contest venue with its own deployment/per-market caps, no visible deep-pocketed professional arbitrage capital, looks structurally like PredictIt (constrained), not Intrade/Betfair (unconstrained). The weight of evidence from the constrained-venue and same-cycle (2024) studies says: **don't expect the edge to mechanically shrink in the final days — the closest analogs show it holding flat or growing.**

## Q2. Volatility, spreads, and liquidity in the final 24-72 hours

**Regime-dependent, not a clean monotonic decay — and the "acute hours" vs. "final days" distinction matters enormously.**

- **Avellaneda & Lipkin (2003)**, "A Market-Induced Mechanism for Stock Pinning," *Quantitative Finance* 3:417-425: when open interest is large and dealers are net **long gamma**, delta-hedging creates a "lensing effect" — volatility **compresses** near expiry.
- **Barbon & Buraschi**, "Gamma Fragility" (WP, 2021 version): the opposite regime — dealers **short gamma** → realized volatility **expands**; a one-SD negative gamma-imbalance shift raises flash-crash probability ~5% relative and is associated with a 20-200bp larger drawdown during crash events, concentrated in **illiquid underlyings**. Net: dealer positioning sign, not calendar proximity, is the real driver — and there's no literal dealer-hedging analog inside prediction markets (flagged as a structural-analogy-only point).
- **SEC Staff Report (Oct. 2021)**, GameStop: spreads **3x** (effective) to **~50x** (nominal) normal; best-bid depth collapsed **4,720 → 19 shares** (>99%); implied vol **9x** typical range; NSCC intraday margin calls hit **$6.9B** in one day ($25.5B total). Explicitly **did not find evidence of a gamma squeeze** — attributed instead to crowding, short interest, and social-media-driven retail attention.
- **Zitzewitz (2026) NBER WP35845**: PredictIt's own academic dataset **excludes the 9-10PM ET hour of Election Night 2020** because the platform was "overwhelmed with traffic" — direct primary-source confirmation of an acute, hours-scale infrastructure/liquidity breakdown concentrated in the live-count window, not a multi-day drift.
- **PredictIt Nov 3-4, 2020 outage** (niche/political-betting press, not confirmed by Tier-1 outlets — treat as credible-but-not-gold-standard): reported **6+ hours** of downtime; prices swung from Trump ~61¢/Biden ~55¢ pre-crash to Biden ~76¢/Trump ~31¢ post-reopen; users reported being unable to adjust positions.
- **Contrasting evidence — scheduled/gradual events behave oppositely:** Kalshi Fed-decision contracts (Krause 2026, secondary-sourced; Bürgi, Deng & Whelan 2025, cited directly inside Zitzewitz's verified text) show **spreads narrowing** as resolution approaches for well-telegraphed, gradual-information events.
- **Lee, Mucklow & Ready (1993)**, *RFS* 6(2):345-374 (secondary-sourced): spreads widen and depth falls **ahead of** scheduled earnings announcements, more so for larger subsequent surprises — the cleanest analog for "spreads widen pre-event," closer to a contested election than a Fed decision.

**Read for SIG:** a genuinely contested midterm race sits closer to the "acute/contested" end of this spectrum (GME, PredictIt 2020) than the "scheduled/gradual" end (Fed decisions). But the hard evidence for liquidity collapse is about **specific acute hours** (live vote-counting, crowding spikes), not a monotonic multi-day deterioration. And critically: **we are not forced to exit at the Nov 4 lock** — we hold to true resolution — so this risk mainly threatens *fill quality on new entries placed during the worst hours*, not forced-liquidation losses on the existing book.

## Q3. Does late information get efficiently priced in, or does lag persist to the end?

**Most important single paper for this entire question:**

- **Rothschild & Pennock (2014)**, "The Extent of Price Misalignment in Prediction Markets," *Algorithmic Finance* 3(1-2):3-20. Direct study of same-event Betfair-vs-Intrade divergence, 2012 U.S. presidential election. **"The size of the price misalignment grows as Election Day approaches, when the markets are more liquid and the opportunity cost of holding the arbitrage shrinks."** The gap **peaked at over 23 percentage points at 8:05 PM ET on Election Day itself.** The two Obama contracts were **>5pp apart for 64 of the final 85 days**. Quantified arbitrage profit: "differences representing between 1 and 5 percent net earnings... can persist for months, even in the face of high liquidity." A real-money field experiment ($3,686 invested in randomized trades) returned **6.38% over 3 months net of all costs**; they estimate the true "shadow" arbitrage opportunity (beyond the visible order book) at **$15,000-$20,000**, roughly **10x** the $1,000-$5,000 visible — i.e., the exploitable gap is larger than it looks from the order book alone. This is the closest documented analogue to our exact strategy (cross-venue short-the-overpriced-side, held to resolution), and the gap was **largest, not smallest, at the literal moment of resolution.**

**Supporting / contextual evidence:**
- **Zitzewitz (2026)**: PredictIt's fee structure scales as roughly (100/9)×(1−H) (H = outcome-probability concentration) — fees bite **hardest in fragmented, longshot-heavy markets**, structurally protecting tail mispricing. ~15% of PredictIt-vs-Betfair paired price observations allowed profitable arbitrage after fees, described as persistent, not one-off.
- **2024 Polymarket-vs-Kalshi gap**: ~8-9pp (Harris 51% Kalshi vs. Trump 53.7%/Harris 45.6% Polymarket) three days after Kalshi's Oct 4, 2024 launch (Sherwood News, Oct 7, 2024) narrowed to roughly **3-6pp by Nov 4 election eve** (Blockonomi) — **partial, incomplete convergence over a month**, not full arbitrage. The "**Théo**" French-trader story (4 linked accounts, **$28.6M** in confirmed pro-Trump bets per Polymarket's own statement, investigated by Nardello & Co. with no manipulation finding) is widely credited with widening Polymarket's pro-Trump lean vs. both Kalshi and polls in the final weeks.
- **Tetlock (2004)**, "How Efficient Are Information Markets? Evidence from an Online Exchange": political/financial contracts on TradeSports are efficient when traders are profit-motivated and unconstrained — but even a real, statistically-significant 4-hours-to-expiry sports overreaction strategy nets only **0.49 ticks/trade after commissions**, i.e., economically marginal once costs are counted even where the bias is real.

**Read for SIG:** the literature's one clean cross-venue arbitrage study (the closest match to what we actually do) found the gap *widens* into resolution. The 2024 real-world precedent shows gaps narrow but don't fully close even by election eve. A smaller, capital-constrained venue is exactly the condition under which correction stalls.

## Q4. Panic-selling/capitulation near deadlines, and quantified liquidity-provider edge

**Strong, well-quantified evidence for the general mechanism in other asset classes; a genuine, explicit gap for prediction markets specifically.**

- **Coval & Stafford (2007)**, "Asset Fire Sales (and Purchases) in Equity Markets," *JFE* 86:479-512: forced mutual-fund selling produces −7.9% to −10.7% price pressure, reversing **+6.1% to +9.65%** over 12-18 months. A calendar-time long-short strategy (long fire-sale names / short inflow-driven purchases) earns **+0.41% to +0.97%/month** (≈5-12%/yr); a strategy that anticipates forced trades before they occur earns "average annual abnormal returns well over 10%." Authors: "investors who trade against constrained mutual funds earn significant returns for providing liquidity."
- **Shleifer & Vishny (1992 JF; 2011 JEP)**: theoretical basis for fire-sale discounts. Important counter-nuance: in **systemic** crises, even well-capitalized arbitrageurs/specialists are themselves constrained and can't fully close the gap — most relevant to market-wide, not single-contract, dislocation (not our situation).
- **Barber, Huang, Odean & Schwarz (2022)**, "Attention-Induced Trading and Returns: Evidence from Robinhood Users," *JF* 77(6):3141-3190 — **the single most directly on-point academic analog**: extreme retail herding produces a same-day abnormal return of **+42%**, reversing to **−9%** over the next month and **−6%** over 5 days (most-extreme events); selling after a herding spike and repurchasing 5 days later earns **+3.5% (+6.4% extreme)**, profitable in **63% of 4,884** herding events, concentrated in **stocks under $1B market cap** (small/illiquid — structurally similar to our thin tail contracts).
- **SEC GameStop Report**: explicitly **debunks** gamma-squeeze as the main driver; documents (citing Glosten & Milgrom 1985) that market makers pay for the right to internalize retail flow specifically because it is less adversely-selected, letting them profit structurally (Citadel Securities internalization went from ~$37M/day to **$4.2B in a single day**, Jan 27, 2021) — no aggregate dollar profit figure given, but the mechanism is well-documented.
- **"Max pain" theory — largely debunked empirically**: a rigorous 919-expiry Deribit study (Cayø Largo, 2026) found max pain is the *closer* predictor of settlement price in only **24.3%** of expiries (should be ~50% if informative; p=2.6×10⁻⁷⁵, significant in the wrong direction) and loses to a naive "price doesn't move" benchmark. One small real effect survives: 1 day pre-expiry, price drifts toward max pain in 55.0% of cases (p=0.003), but covers only 13% of the gap and fades to nothing by 6 hours out.
- **Prediction-market-specific panic-selling**: evidence is thin. PredictIt's 2020 election-night crash is real but only niche-press-documented. Polymarket's "Théo" trade ($28-30M staked, an estimated **$48-85M profit**) is the best-documented deadline-adjacent prediction-market profit — but it's a **directional conviction trade against crowd/polls**, not a "fade the panic-sold flow" trade; mechanism differs from our stated strategy. **No credible, incident-specific journalism was found for Kalshi on election night 2024** — an explicit gap, not a finding. Both Polymarket and Kalshi reportedly saw **93-98%** DAU drops immediately post-election (secondary source, moderate confidence) — consistent with liquidity/participation being sharply concentrated in the deadline window itself and then evaporating.

**Read for SIG:** no paper directly quantifies "edge from fading prediction-market panic-selling near a deadline" — treat this as an open gap your own trading data is filling, not confirming. But every verified analog (fire sales, Robinhood herding) shows a real, repeatable, quantified premium (3.5%–12%+ annualized/event-based) for the **disciplined, non-forced counterparty** who absorbs urgency-driven flow — consistent with, though not proof of, our thesis.

## Synthesis and recommendation

1. **The decisive variable is capital-constraint, not time.** Every source that found FLB/mispricing shrinking near resolution (Page & Clemen/Intrade, Erikson-Wlezien/IEM-vs-polls) studied **unconstrained** arbitrage capital. Every source most structurally like SIG — a capped, thinner venue (Zitzewitz/PredictIt; Rothschild-Pennock's own cross-exchange friction result; Clinton & Huang's 2024 cross-venue arbitrage) — found mispricing **flat or growing** into the final stretch, peaking at or near the moment of resolution itself (23pp gap at 8:05pm ET on 2012 Election Day). SIG's own per-market/deployment caps and apparent lack of deep professional arb capital put it in the "persists/grows" category, not the "shrinks" category.
2. **Liquidity risk is real but narrowly scoped.** The hard evidence (SEC/GME, PredictIt's own excluded data-hour) is about **acute, hours-scale breakdowns during live/contested counting**, not a gradual multi-day decay — and scheduled/gradual-information event contracts (Kalshi Fed decisions) show spreads *narrowing*, not widening, into resolution. Since our strategy holds to true resolution rather than forced-exiting at the Nov 4 lock, this risk threatens **entry fill quality**, not a forced-liquidation loss.
3. **This argues for net MORE aggressive, deployed EARLY in the remaining window, via WIDER COVERAGE rather than concentration.** Concretely:
   - Don't hold back capital "for the final days" expecting bigger edge then — the strongest evidence says the edge is already as large as it will get, or still growing, not something to wait for.
   - Don't wait until the literal final 24-48h to place new orders — that's when acute infrastructure/liquidity risk concentrates (election-night-style congestion); get new entries on with a buffer before that window.
   - Prioritize **reaching into more, smaller, illiquid tail markets** (your own prior finding that RelValue's activity-based ranking biases away from the very illiquid tails where edge concentrates is directly consistent with Rothschild-Pennock's finding that the *real* opportunity is ~10x the visible order book) over just increasing size on already-held positions, which are also capped per-market anyway.
   - Keep your own existing kill-switch/drawdown governor as the binding constraint — nothing here overrides your own internal risk system; it argues for pace and coverage, not for discarding risk limits.
4. **Reconciling with the prior internal "convergence agent" finding** (checkpoint 003): its premise — informed flow concentrates near resolution — is well-supported (Rhode-Strumpf late money, Ottaviani-Sørensen theory, Clinton-Huang's arbitrage-grows-into-final-two-weeks finding). But the strongest same-structure evidence argues this should mean the **gap persists/grows**, not that you should defer or shrink longshot-side size. The "scale in over the window, don't wait for the final week" half of that advice is well-supported; the "reduce longshot-side size in the final 1-2 weeks" half is **not** supported by the external literature reviewed here and should be revisited against your own markout data rather than treated as a settled external-literature conclusion.

## Honest gaps / what would change this conclusion

- **No study matches your exact structure**: a trading-deadline-before-true-resolution contest market on a **midterm** (not presidential) cycle. All the hardest cross-venue/liquidity numbers (Rothschild-Pennock 2012, Clinton-Huang/Cardozo 2024, Zitzewitz/PredictIt 2020, SEC/GME) are presidential-cycle or mega-cap-stock events with far more attention/liquidity than individual House/Senate contracts will see.
- Shin's "bias widens dynamically near post-time" causal claim is **unverified** against primary text (paywalled) — treat as plausible, not confirmed.
- Reichenbach & Walther (2025, SSRN, blocked at fetch) reportedly find a U-shaped miscalibration (early-life **and** near-resolution) with "no sustained longshot bias" in aggregate on Polymarket — a potential counter-data-point that could not be independently verified; worth a follow-up if time permits.
- Kalshi-specific election-night liquidity/panic evidence is an outright gap — no credible incident-level journalism found despite multiple targeted searches.
- No paper directly quantifies a "fade retail panic in prediction markets near a deadline" edge — this is inferred by analogy (fire sales, Robinhood herding), not directly demonstrated in this asset class.
